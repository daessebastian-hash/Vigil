        Introduction

        “Amazon History of a Former Nail Salon Worker” tells the story of a mother’s final years entirely through her online order history, moving month by month from nail supplies, and finally leading towards, chemotherapy supplies, and finally memorial and death purchases. As both close reading and computational analysis reveal, the poem’s grief lives less in what it says than in grammar, by stripping away verbs, pronouns, and time words, Vuong makes reader experience the same feeling of consuming the illness does by taking the life away from her, forcing us to rebuild a whole human life from the objects she leaves behind.

        Computational analysis

        While my close reading traced how “Amazon History of a Former Nail Salon Worker” tells a mother’s decline through the objects she buys, a computational analysis reveals that this decline even without what the grammar doesn’t supply is still carried just as powerfully. Counted by part of speech, the poem is roughly 82% nouns and contains almost no verbs. The corpus comparison further shows that ordinary words like her, his, and they never appear. The poem uses only two prepositions, and both are “in”. Not one of them points to time, which is strange, because the whole poem is arranged like a calendar. Lines like “Eternity Aluminum Urn, Dove and Rose engraved, small.” Nobody grieves in it. Nothing is mourned. Theres not after to tell us someone had died. It’s just a product, a finish, and a size. This further proves the thesis; it shows me where the feeling comes from. Close reading lets us feel the loss when the poem goes from nail supplies to a memorial plaque. The data demonstrates how the loss embeds itself in the story, the poem takes away the people, the pronouns, and the time words that a person normally lives inside when she speaks. What’s left is a woman who doesn’t do or feel anything on the page. She is just a stack of nouns and brand names. So, the list isn’t just a container for the story it is the very argument. In its own grammar, the poem does the very thing it describes, it shrinks a person down to an object or objects they leave behind.

        Close reading

        The Amazon History of a Former Nail Salon Worker shows how ordinary objects can preserve the story of a mother’s life and make her absence felt after death. The shopping-list form at the beginning presents a normal routine through nail supplies, food, and clothing. Because these objects suggest work, personal interests, and family relationships, they establish the mother as a person with a full life before illness becomes the focus. As the monthly entries progress, everyday purchases give way to a walker and chemotherapy effects. This chronological shift reveals her declining health without directly describing her experience, making the readers piece the story together. The strongest contrast occurs when a birthday card promising that mother and son will always be together is followed by an urn next month revealing how life may be taken at an instant and is devastating especially when there is hope for life. The placement of the card reassures this message of pain, while suggesting that their connection will last an eternity through loving memory. Through these purchases, the poem turns an ordinary record of consumption into a record of fighting through tough moments, pain, and especially love.

        Conclusion

        Together, the close reading and the Computational Analysis show the same thing, the poem lists everything the mother buys, but it leaves out who she was. Vuong uses a cold, simple form, a shopping record, and lets what’s missing carry sadness. The lack of verbs and pronouns doesn’t mean the poem has no feeling. Instead, it makes readers fill in love and loss by themselves. In the end, the poem shows that receipts, order histories, and data can track a person’s life but never truly capture who the person is, that’s what makes this style very interesting because it forces the reader to fill in for who this person is and will make a reader feel a much stronger connection to the poem as they are the ones connecting to whoever they make the person to be. This is a great writing method, but the reader also may never be able to truly capture the love and grief the author wants the reader to see.
